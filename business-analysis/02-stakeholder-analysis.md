# Stakeholder Analysis

## Purpose

This stakeholder analysis identifies the groups that would influence, use or be affected by a proposed reservation-monitoring process. As this is an independent portfolio case study, the stakeholders and their needs are assumed and would require validation during a real discovery phase.

## Stakeholder Matrix

| Stakeholder | Role in the Process | Influence | Interest | Assumed Needs and Concerns | Proposed Engagement |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| General Manager | Project sponsor and owner of the overall business outcome | High | High | Lower operational uncertainty, improved occupancy forecasting and measurable business value | Regular progress reviews and approval of scope, priorities and pilot |
| Reservations Manager | Operational owner of the reservation and follow-up process | High | High | Clear risk indicators, manageable staff workload and fewer unnecessary interventions | Discovery interviews, process-mapping workshop, requirements validation and UAT |
| Reservations Staff | Daily users of the proposed process | Medium | High | Simple workflow, explainable alerts, accurate information and limited additional administration | Observation, interviews, prototype feedback, training and UAT |
| Revenue Manager | Uses booking patterns to support pricing and inventory decisions | High | High | Reliable cancellation forecasts, segment-level reporting and visibility of potential revenue exposure | KPI workshops, dashboard validation and pilot evaluation |
| Finance Team | Reviews revenue performance and financial exposure | Medium | Medium | Transparent financial measures and clear distinction between potential exposure and confirmed loss | Requirements review and validation of financial definitions |
| Marketing Team | Manages customer segments, campaigns and booking channels | Medium | Medium | Evidence on customer and channel behaviour without unnecessary restrictions on valuable customers | Findings review and consultation on customer communication |
| Hotel Guests | Receive confirmation requests, reminders or revised booking communications | Low | High | Transparent policies, convenient communication, privacy and fair treatment | Customer research, feedback surveys and pilot monitoring |
| IT or System Provider | Configures or integrates the proposed solution | High | Medium | Clear requirements, data definitions, security rules and integration constraints | Technical workshops, feasibility assessment and solution design |
| Data Protection or Compliance Representative | Advises on the use of customer and booking information | High | Medium | Lawful, proportionate and secure use of personal information | Privacy review before design approval and pilot implementation |
| Online Travel Agencies | External booking channels providing reservation information | Medium | Medium | Accurate inventory updates and compatibility with existing booking arrangements | Integration review and contractual consultation where required |

## Power and Interest Assessment

### Manage Closely

- General Manager
- Reservations Manager
- Revenue Manager

These stakeholders have both high influence and a strong interest in the outcome. They should participate in major scope, requirements and prioritisation decisions.

### Keep Satisfied

- IT or System Provider
- Data Protection or Compliance Representative

These stakeholders have significant influence over feasibility and approval. They should be consulted at important design and implementation stages.

### Keep Informed

- Reservations Staff
- Finance Team
- Marketing Team
- Hotel Guests

These groups are affected by the change or provide important operational and customer information. Their feedback should be incorporated throughout discovery, testing and evaluation.

### Monitor and Consult When Relevant

- Online Travel Agencies

Their involvement will depend on whether the proposed process requires technical, data or contractual changes affecting external booking channels.

## Stakeholder Conflicts to Manage

Potential conflicts may include:

- Management may want stronger cancellation controls, while marketing may be concerned about reducing booking conversion.
- Reservations staff may benefit from better prioritisation but may resist a process that creates additional manual work.
- Revenue management may request detailed customer-level indicators, while compliance stakeholders may require data minimisation.
- Customers may value flexible booking terms, while the hotel may seek stronger confirmation or deposit requirements.
- IT may prefer a technically simple solution, while operational users may request more detailed functionality.

These conflicts should be explored through workshops, evidence review and agreed decision criteria rather than resolved through assumptions.

## Initial Discovery Plan

| Activity | Participants | Intended Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor interview | General Manager | Confirm the problem, objectives, scope and measures of success |
| Process-mapping workshop | Reservations Manager and Reservations Staff | Document the current reservation and cancellation process |
| Data and KPI workshop | Revenue Manager and Finance Team | Agree metric definitions, baselines and reporting needs |
| Customer-impact workshop | Marketing, Reservations and Compliance | Evaluate communication, fairness and privacy implications |
| Technical discovery workshop | IT or System Provider and operational representatives | Assess system capabilities, integrations and constraints |
| Requirements validation session | All key decision-makers and user representatives | Review, prioritise and approve requirements |
